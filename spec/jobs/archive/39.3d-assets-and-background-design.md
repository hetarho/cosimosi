**Background shader work — as-built [T003/T008]**

The emotion backdrop is a **TSL sky-sphere**: `@cosimosi/3d-renderer`'s `SkySphere` layer draws a `BackSide`
sphere enclosing the scene, shaded on its inner surface, its color sampled from the emotion palette via the
`assets/sky/emotion-gradient` ramp texture (a real 3D body wrapping the universe, not a screen-space wash).

**Four composable axes, twelve recipes.** Backgrounds are the product's core revenue surface and must grow
without bound, so the catalogue is a set of ARRANGEMENTS rather than a set of hand-written shaders. A primitive
added to an axis multiplies across every recipe; a trick inlined into one recipe helps one recipe. react-bits was
where the looks were found, not something tracked — there is no `fidelity` field and no notion of a source to be
faithful to.

| axis        | module                            | what it offers                                                                                                                       |
| ----------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **domain**  | `assets/sky/sky-domain.ts`        | `skyDir` · `skyBand` · `skyAngleTo` · `skyTangentFrame`/`skyLocal2D` · `skyCells`/`skyCellEdge` · `skyCloud`/`skyRidge` · `skyDrift` |
| **field**   | `shader-art/noise.ts`, `field.ts` | fbm · ridged · worley · gnoise · domain warp                                                                                         |
| **emotion** | `assets/sky/sky-emotion.ts`       | `emotionAnchors` · `emotionRadius` · `emotionField` (share · colorOf · presence)                                                     |
| **finish**  | `assets/sky/sky-finish.ts`        | `skyBandLine` · `skyRing` · `skyQuantize` · `markSize` · `skyHeadroom`/`skyFinish` · `skyOver` · `skyVoid`                           |

A recipe (`assets/sky/*-sky.ts`, registered in `sky-effects.ts`) is a pure colour-node builder
`<name>SkyNode({ gradient, time, count, weights, headroom })` that arranges those four and owns no colour of its
own. `ripple-grid` is deleted and `grainstorm` added; twelve remain.

- **No chart, anywhere.** Every domain is a function of the surface DIRECTION alone. A chart that flattens the
  sphere onto a plane must gather it somewhere, and the gather is visible as a pinch — a sheet of paper drawn to a
  point. `skyStereo` was that chart and is **removed**; the four recipes that read it (iridescence ·
  floating-lines · pixel-blast · evil-eye) are rebuilt on direction domains, so the artifact is now
  unrepresentable rather than merely avoided. Where a recipe needs local 2D coordinates around a feature it uses
  `skyLocal2D`, whose one degenerate point is the antipode — a hemisphere from the feature it describes.
- **Weight buys area, never depth.** A spherical cap of angular radius `acos(1 − 2·w)` covers exactly `w` of the
  sphere, and those radii sum to the whole sphere across a normalized weight set (guarded to 10 places in
  `sky-emotion.test.ts`). `emotionField` blends the feelings' colours by normalized share, so a faintly-weighted
  feeling is at **full chroma** wherever it wins. The ramp's `DEPTH_CURVE` — which mixed low-weight colours
  toward the night base as `(share/topShare)^1.5`, turning a 3% feeling into mud — is **removed**: every band now
  reaches the same exposure and weight decides only its width.
- **Anchors are a lattice, not a ring.** Features are placed by the Fibonacci lattice, and a feature's SIZE comes
  from its own weight — never from the count or from the gap to its neighbours. Thirteen feelings are thirteen
  ordinary places.
- **A per-emotion feature reads its own band.** `colorOf(i)` samples the centre of feeling `i`'s band
  (`emotionRampCenters`), not `i / (count − 1)`: even spacing along the ramp is not where the bands are, so with
  unequal weights several such samples land inside the widest band and one feeling's colour appears on several
  features at once.
- **Size, not opacity.** A feeling's strength is spent on a mark's width or radius. Drawn at low alpha a faint
  feeling does not read as faint — it reads as absent, then as flickering as it crosses visibility.
- **The sky leaves headroom.** Stars, the nebula field and the bloom pass all ADD light over the sky
  (`PostFX`: `scenePass.rgb.add(bloomPass.rgb)`), and addition over a bright surface passes 1 in every channel at
  once — which is white, and is why a star against a lit curtain lost its colour. Each recipe holds itself under a
  ceiling (`rendering.emotion_sky_headroom.*`, **(gen:values)**) applied as a soft roll-off in `skyFinish`, so
  structure survives at the top of the range. Sparse mark recipes barely need one (0.84–0.88); full-frame curtains
  and fluids need the most (soft-aurora 0.58). **The shared compositing path is untouched** — the star, nebula and
  latent looks tuned across S1–S6 are unchanged.
- **`SkySphere` takes an `effect` prop**; it repaints the ramp on emotion change and rebuilds the material only
  on effect change (each GPU resource disposed per-change, never cross-disposing the shared gradient/geometry),
  and passes the recipe's `headroom` into the builder.
- The earlier **2D raw-WebGL2** exploration is **removed** — `three` stays confined to `@cosimosi/3d-renderer`;
  no raw GLSL/WebGL in app slices.

**Performance floors — as-measured [T012]**

Driven in headless Chrome over CDP against the running `/design` Universe group — the first time this job's
graphs met a real GPU. Reproduced by `scripts`-free probes kept out of the repo; the numbers below are what
they returned.

- **Backend: Apple Metal 3** (`navigator.gpu.requestAdapter()` → vendor `apple`, architecture `metal-3`) —
  hardware, not the SwiftShader software fallback that would make any timing meaningless.
- **WGSL compiles.** Every canvas on the page reported a live `webgpu` context, and cycling **all twelve
  sky recipes** and **all ten star shapes** produced **zero** shader, pipeline or exception log entries.
  A TSL graph that fails to build shows only as a blank canvas otherwise, which is why this is driven
  rather than asserted.
- **GLSL compiles.** The nebula panel's fallback toggle flipped that canvas to `webgl2` on a real remount
  (label `WebGL2 폴백 강제` → `WebGPU 사용`), again with zero shader complaints, and the captured frame shows
  the starfield and the emotion kernels drawing — not an empty surface.
- **Frame budget.** At a 2880×1800 backing store with four live canvases on the page, frame intervals ran
  **8.3 ms median, 9.2 ms p95, 9.5 ms worst** — vsync-locked at the display's 120 Hz, i.e. the work fits
  inside a 120 Hz budget with headroom rather than merely inside 60. Measured identically on all five
  probes, including the star bench at 3× size and the forgetting row.
- **Instancing intact.** No per-node mesh exists anywhere: the bodies draw through `InstancedNodeLayer`'s
  single `InstancedMesh`, and `StarField` · `LatentField` · `ColorField` each keep their own one.
- **`rendering.max_pixel_ratio` honoured** by all eleven canvases in both apps — every `UniverseCanvas`
  mount passes `dpr={[1, VALUES.rendering.maxPixelRatio]}`.
- **`three` stays confined** to `@cosimosi/3d-renderer`: no `from 'three…'` import exists anywhere else in
  `apps/**` or `packages/**`.
- **No coordinates are set here.** No app slice writes a node position; the layers read the force-sim
  buffer.
- **The sweep's sky acceleration costs nothing.** Running the seconds uniform faster is the same graph
  evaluated at the same rate — the shader does not get more expensive because its input moves quicker.

**Still unverified: React Native on a device.** The measurements above are the web canvas. The RN surface
shares the one TSL source and its own `/design` Universe group exists to be read on hardware, but no
device pass has run — the mobile frame budget (the nebula's two-octave cloud especially) stays open.

**A finding for the T013 round, not a regression:** with the emotion capacity removed, the star bench hands

score — whether a sky holding every feeling at once is a sky worth wearing — and it is a reviewer's call
about the default rather than a defect in the partition. Verified headlessly: the emotion
partition's arithmetic, the ramp's equal-depth bands, `pnpm lint`, every package test, the web build and the

**Decision — the free entry point, and a backdrop's emotion capacity (2026-07-28).** The reviewer chose the two
defaults the registries open on, and changed what an effect's emotion count MEANS.

- **`DEFAULT_STAR_SHAPE = 'facet'`** (다듬은 별빛 — twenty quiet faces, a small hand-cut moon). `orb` stays in the

- **No sky caps how many feelings it takes.** An emotion capacity was authored, then removed once the emotion axis
  landed: the partition divides the sphere by weight, so a sky handed thirteen feelings shows thirteen smaller
  territories rather than a muddier version of five. A cap would only re-decide, per recipe, something the
  partition already answers correctly for all of them. The showcase's count row is a review convenience, not a
  property of any sky.
- **`grainient` opens the universe, `grainstorm` sits beside it.** One feeling washed over everything is the
  quietest the registry can be and the right thing to open on. Grainstorm is the same marble **printed**: grain as

  from the midpoint, a coarse second grain layer sampled off the same warped frame so the tooth travels with the
  marble). The two are separate recipes because grain and colour have to move together — heavy grain laid over a
  soft gradient eats the very thing that makes the gradient readable.

## Emotion palette values — as-built [T005]

The default `mood → color` table (`packages/emotion/src/palette.ts`) is **re-authored in OkLCH on the 2D
language's own perceptual lightness scale** (`packages/ui/src/palette.ts`) — every one of the thirteen colours
lands on step 300 (L 0.80), 400 (0.72) or 500 (0.63). That is what makes A4 structural rather than a promise:
2D chrome and the 3D bodies read the same table through the one `moodColor` seam, and the emotion colours now
sit on the same ladder the chrome's ramps do. Three channels, each with one job:

- **hue = identity.** The warm arc (rose → coral → amber → gold → green) is pleasant, the cool arc (teal →
  blue → violet → magenta) unpleasant — the reading `checkPaletteAxisConsistency` guards.
- **chroma = vividness.** ANGER is the most saturated colour in the table (C 0.20); NEUTRAL is almost
  colourless (C 0.012); TIRED and EMPTINESS are deliberately muted.
- **lightness follows the HUE, never the feeling.** Each hue sits on the step where it is most itself (yellow
  is only gold when light, magenta only crimson when deep). Emotion may not spend the **brightness** channel —
  a star's brightness carries its strength (plan 24), so two memories of equal strength must not differ in
  luminance because of their mood. This is why the palette is not "bright = happy".

Verified numerically before landing: all thirteen inside sRGB, **axis-consistent** with margin (worst severity
0.377 vs the 0.6 threshold — ANGER's crimson, by design), **13/13 unique** with a minimum separation of
**ΔE(OkLab) 0.0545** (closest pair TIRED↔EMPTINESS), and legible as lights on the emotion skin's bare night
(contrast ≥ 4.8:1 against `#0a0a12`). Two of those are now **guarded in `palette.test.ts`** — step membership
and pairwise separation — so the design cannot drift silently. The mapping structure, the meaning layer and
`spec/values.yaml` are untouched ([I11]); only the colour content moved.

Found while measuring, **not fixed here**: the registry's alternative `muted-dusk` palette (plan 51 / job 62)
has ANGER↔STRESS at ΔE 0.024 — the two read as one colour. It passes the axis guardrail, so it is a legibility
finding for the reviewer, not a regression of this job's default.

All of it landed: the palette is the one table both languages read, the chosen sky wears the real universe
background through the skin's `sky` seam (the flat gradient/nebula registry, `Background` and the unused
`UniverseScene` are gone), the TSL source runs on the RN canvas, and the sign-off is recorded in
design-language §21.

## The other bodies — as-built [T007]

The four bodies Epic A shipped against placeholders now each say something, and what they say is what
they ARE. The channels, layers, ports and coordinate seams are untouched — this is finish, not
plumbing — and nothing here reads the domain or sets a position.

- **Cell-star (Neuron) — a membrane, and it does not move.** `cell-star-body.ts` shades its interior
  down under its own silhouette (a view-space grazing rim, so it holds from any camera angle without a
  light in the scene), which reads as a small cell holding something rather than a plastic dot. Still
  seedless and still emotion-free [V5][I3]. And it is deliberately **motionless**: the memory body
  breathes, the neuron holds. What a universe feels is alive; what it knows is stable.
- **Filament (Synapse) — a cord of light that pulses inward from both ends.** The ribbon's centre line
  now carries the colour and both rims fall to nothing (`FILAMENT_VERTEX_EDGE`: side across the width,
  along between the endpoints, plus a per-edge phase — static geometry data, built once by
  `buildFilamentFrame`, so the strand costs no per-frame work beyond its endpoints). A thick synapse
  reads as a bright cord, a thin one as a thread. The shimmer travels **inward from A and B together**:
  a synapse joins neuron A and neuron B, not a from and a to, so nothing may flow one way along it and
  invent a direction the domain never stored.
- **Latent field — dust, not beads.** Each mote fades at its own silhouette and composites additively,
  so a clump pools into a faint haze instead of stacking hard dots, and each **breathes on its own slow
  phase** (an `instanceIndex` hash, the same one the background field twinkles on). Every mote keeps
  one colour and one size — a latent neuron has no identity and no emotion yet [E7a][V7] — so the only
  thing distinguishing one is when it happens to be brightest.
- **Nebula — gas, not a lens flare.** `ColorField` multiplies its mid-band by a low-frequency cloud
  sampled in **world space**, so the structure belongs to the region of the universe rather than to the
  screen: the camera moves through it and two neighbouring contributors agree where the cloud is thick.
  The band mask holds the cloud off both the centre and the rim, so the peak stays exactly on the
  contributor's coordinate and the silhouette still fades before its own edge — the softness and
  centring the earlier rounds bought are preserved by construction.

`reducedMotion` now reaches the strand shimmer and the dust breath (web + mobile mounts and the `/test`
universe panel), so the whole scene freezes together rather than one layer at a time.

Verified: the four graphs build headlessly (`ambient-bodies.test.ts` — a TSL graph that throws only
shows as a blank canvas otherwise), the ribbon frame matches the layer's corner order and gives every
edge its own deterministic phase, `pnpm lint` · all package/app tests · web build · mobile typecheck are
green. **Not yet verified: the on-device/GPU pass** — no WebGPU/WebGL2 compile-and-look was possible in
this session, so the frame budget of the nebula's 2-octave cloud on mobile and the four looks themselves
are open for the T013 round.
