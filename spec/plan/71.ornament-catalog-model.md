# 71. ornament catalog model

> Ornament catalog model — Epic N's root: a NEW `internal/store` context owning `Ornament`, `OrnamentKind`
> (`BACKGROUND`/`STAR_SHADER`/`PALETTE`), `OrnamentOwnership` and `OrnamentSelection` (one row per kind), the in-code
> catalog whose membership **derives from the shipped renderer registries and never declares a count**, whose
> purchasable rows price **by kind** and never per row, the two tables, and the `store.v1` read contract. The
> invariant that shapes everything: an ornament sells a **render-parameter id and nothing else** — no row, struct or
> proto message has a field for a color, size, brightness or seed, so [V10]/[I11] hold structurally. It leaves
> `Decorate` (the write) and every pixel of UI to siblings. Scope: FS | Status: planning
>
> Layer D. Home: `apps/api/internal/store/{doc.go,types.go,catalog.go,service.go,ports.go,errors.go}` + `store/pg` +
> `store/rpc`, `proto/cosimosi/store/v1/store.proto`, migration `00021_store_ornaments.sql`, plus the two renderer
> selection seams the catalog addresses (`packages/3d-renderer/src/assets/sky/sky-effects.ts` ·
> `assets/bodies/star-shapes.ts`). It fills the "no user preference for the skin" seam
> [14.rendering-foundation](14.rendering-foundation.md) left open and leaves the palette-storage cutover and the
> purchase transaction to [72](72.decorate-usecase.md), the panel to [73](73.decoration-panel.md).

## Purpose

cosimosi's universe is the user's own; [P4] promises they may dress it, and [P5]–[P11] make that promise concrete in v2
as 우주 꾸미기. But the product has nowhere to put an ornament today. The shipped renderer publishes three id
registries — the emotion-sky effects (`packages/3d-renderer/src/assets/sky/sky-effects.ts` `SKY_EFFECTS`, of which
only the authored default is reachable in production), the star shapes (`assets/bodies/star-shapes.ts` `STAR_SHAPES`,
default `orb`, user-selectable nowhere), and the palettes (`packages/emotion/src/registry.ts` `PALETTES`, whose
per-user choice lives in `account`'s `palette_preferences`) — plus a skin seam (`SkinProvider`, `setSkinKey`) with
**zero production callers** of the runtime setter. None of these is a catalog, none knows what a purchase is, and
there is no notion of owning anything. This unit is the domain that closes that gap: what an ornament **is**, which
ones exist, what a user **owns**, and what is **applied right now**.

The unit exists to make one thing impossible rather than discouraged. [I11] says money and achievement cannot buy
meaning: no purchase may move a star, change its brightness, recolor its emotion, or reshape its seed. [V10] says the
decoration layer is _render parameters only_. The weakest way to honor that is a review rule over a `params JSONB`
column. The way taken here is that **the catalog row, the DB row, and the proto message have no field in which a render
parameter's _value_ could be written** — they carry an opaque id, a kind and an acquisition path, nothing more. The
color table, the shader graph and the sky node stay where they already are, in the rendering layer behind the plan-17
`moodColor` seam and the plan-14 asset sources ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md) §3.4). The server never learns what
an ornament looks like.

The second shaping requirement is [P7]: 보관함·인벤토리·장착 must not exist as a user concept. That is enforced here as
absence — the contract has no `ListInventory` message and no `Equip` method, and `GetCatalog` answers with
per-item `owned`/`selected` booleans so the panel can render one list of everything ([P6]) and reveal ownership through
price alone ([P7]).

Finally, the catalog is deliberately **a wrapper, not a parallel asset library — and it never declares a count.**
`BACKGROUND` wraps whatever `SKY_EFFECTS` publishes, `STAR_SHADER` wraps whatever `STAR_SHAPES` publishes (both are
[57.3d-assets-and-background-design](57.3d-assets-and-background-design.md)'s output and still under its design
sign-off), and `PALETTE` wraps whatever `PALETTES` publishes. Membership is read off the registries at implement
time, the way `PALETTES`' own comment already puts it: "the count is this record's length, never a declared number."
The scope line is FS rather than BE because the two renderer selection seams are still one-value: the sky effect is
hardwired into the single `emotion` skin, and `StarLayer` builds only the default body in a bare `useMemo` — this
unit widens both into per-user selections.

**Non-goals**

- **No `Decorate`, no purchase, no spend.** The one-transaction buy-and-apply, the `store.SpendGate` port, the
  `ornament_purchase` ledger reason, the `STORE_INSUFFICIENT_TWINKLE` reason, and the
  `palette_preferences → ornament_selections` migration + `DROP TABLE` all belong to
  [72.decorate-usecase](72.decorate-usecase.md). This unit ships the tables, the queries, the catalog
  and the reads that `Decorate` composes.
- **No UI, no preview, no copy.** The over-canvas panel, the in-memory preview store, the floating save button and every
  `store_*` i18n key are [73.decoration-panel](73.decoration-panel.md). The
  `inventory|equip|장착|보관함` lint rule that mechanically enforces [P7] in FE source is
  [65.twinkle-kind-redefinition](65.twinkle-kind-redefinition.md)'s.
- **No achievement catalog and no reward payout.** Which achievement grants which ornament, `ClaimAchievement`, and the
  `OrnamentGranter` binding are [74](74.achievement-catalog-model.md)/[75](75.achievement-tracking-usecase.md).
  This unit only declares that an ornament may be `ACHIEVEMENT`-acquired ([P11]).
- **No new visual assets and no aesthetic authoring.** Star-shape looks, per-effect tuning and every background's
  finish are reviewed under [57.3d-assets-and-background-design](57.3d-assets-and-background-design.md), which also
  owns both registries' contents; this unit consumes the id vocabulary and pins the permitted parameter axes as
  guards.
- **No new palettes.** The `PALETTE` kind wraps whatever `packages/emotion` publishes; authoring palette tables stays
  [51.palette-customization](51.palette-customization.md)'s registry, and palette color tables remain code content.
- **No bloom, no camera, no post.** `BloomParams` and `camera.fov` stay skin-level scene defaults **outside the
  catalog** — permanently, in every kind. A lower bloom threshold makes every star read brighter, i.e. it
  visually undoes forgetting, which is exactly what [I11] forbids.
- **No `users` FK and no signup grant.** A `FREE` row is owned by construction, resolved as **absence of
  a row**; there is no backfill and no "grant the default palette at signup" step ([P10] as amended has no 무상 소유
  지급 left to implement — palettes carry no ownership at all).

## Design

### Placement — a NEW `internal/store` supporting context (ARCHITECTURE §2.2/§2.3)

Ornaments have their own aggregate (`OrnamentSelection` per user, with `OrnamentOwnership` as its permanent history),
their own lifecycle (acquire once, select any time) and their own tables. Folding them into `memory` would put
product-store vocabulary inside the one neuroscience core context; folding them into `account` would make the palette a
preference again and make [P8]'s one transaction across all three kinds impossible (§2.2 forbids `store` reaching into
`account`'s table, and spanning both would make one context the transaction owner of the other's tables). So: a peer
supporting context, following the observed `twinkle`/`admin` recipe exactly —

```
apps/api/internal/store/{doc.go, types.go, catalog.go, service.go, ports.go, errors.go}
apps/api/internal/store/pg/store.go            the only package importing dbgen (§2.6)
apps/api/internal/store/rpc/{server.go, reasons.go}
apps/api/cmd/api/store.go                      storeServiceOption(...) → platform.WithRPCService
apps/api/db/queries/store/ornaments.sql        directory name == context name (the isolation gate keys on it)
```

`store.NewService(store.ServiceDeps{Ownerships: …, Selections: …})` — the `twinkle.ServiceDeps` form, since there is
more than one dep. No empty `domain/`/`app/` directories (§2.9 #2). `store` imports **no** other `internal/` context;
the only sanctioned internal→internal import in the repo stays `ai → memory`.

### The catalog — code, not a table; membership derives from the registries; price is by kind

The catalog is a package-level Go table in `store/catalog.go`: `map[OrnamentID]Ornament`, **generated row-for-row from
the registry id fixture** (one row per registry key, see the drift guard below), never hand-counted. Kinds and
acquisition paths are **content**, so they are code; the kind-price _numbers_ are tuning, so they are `values.yaml`.
PRD §5.9/§5.10's "오너먼트 가격 → values.yaml" is amended by this unit to that split.

```go
type OrnamentID string            // opaque to the server: "<kind-slug>.<registry-key>"
type OrnamentKind uint8           // BACKGROUND | STAR_SHADER | PALETTE
type OrnamentAcquisition uint8    // FREE | PURCHASE | ACHIEVEMENT

type Ornament struct {
    ID          OrnamentID
    Kind        OrnamentKind
    Acquisition OrnamentAcquisition
}
```

Three fields, all discrete. **There is no `Price int` field and no per-row tier field** — a `PURCHASE` row's price is
resolved from its `Kind` (`values.StoreBackgroundPrice` / `values.StoreStarShaderPrice`), so a per-row literal price is
unrepresentable, which is how the pricing rule becomes structural rather than a review note. **There is no `Label`,
`Blurb`, `PreviewURL`, `Color`, `Size`, `Brightness`, `Seed` or `Params` field** — the server cannot describe an
ornament, only name one. Display names are FE-side keys keyed by ornament id (plan 73); the renderer registries keep
their own developer-facing labels alongside the shipped `SKY_EFFECTS` `label`/`blurb` fields.

`OrnamentID` values are literal strings of the form `<lower(kind)>.<registry key>` — `background.grainient`,
`star_shader.orb`, `palette.cosimosi-default`. Two consequences are load-bearing: a duplicate id is a **compile
error** (a duplicate constant key in a Go map literal), and cross-kind uniqueness — which `ornament_ownerships`' primary
key `(user_id, ornament_id)` depends on — follows from the prefix rather than from a test. The Go side never parses the
id; the FE mirror splits on the first `.` to pick the registry.

**Catalog membership — a rule, never a count.** The rows derive from the registries at implement time:

| Kind          | Rows (derived)                          | Free default (the registry's own)         | Achievement-only                             | Everything else                |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `BACKGROUND`  | one per `SkyEffectKey` in `SKY_EFFECTS` | `DEFAULT_SKY_EFFECT` (today `grainient`)  | exactly **one** row, id pinned at job-author | `PURCHASE` at the kind price   |
| `STAR_SHADER` | one per `StarShapeKey` in `STAR_SHAPES` | `DEFAULT_STAR_SHAPE` (today `orb`)        | exactly **one** row, id pinned at job-author | `PURCHASE` at the kind price   |
| `PALETTE`     | one per id in `PALETTES`                | `DEFAULT_PALETTE_ID` (`cosimosi-default`) | — (never)                                    | `FREE` — every palette ([P10]) |

Exactly **two** achievement-only rows — one background, one star shape — keeps [P11]'s "아주 가끔" honest and pairs
1:1 with plan 74's two ornament capstones. Their concrete ids are **pinned at `/create-plan-job 71`, after plan 57's
design sign-off** — a plan never pins a registry id the sign-off may still drop or rename. The rows' aesthetics are
reviewed under plan 57; the registries' sizes are read, never asserted.

**Cross-catalog direction is one-way:** `achievement` (plan 74) names an ornament id as a reward; `store` **never names
an achievement**. `Ornament` therefore has no `AchievementID` field and the proto has no `achievement_id`, so there is no
id to drift. The panel joins an `ACHIEVEMENT` item to its condition text through `ListAchievements`, which the `/me`
achievements tab already reads. Plan 74 lands the pairing test (`apps/api/cmd/api/catalog_consistency_test.go` —
`cmd/api` is the one package that legitimately sees both catalogs, §2.4), asserting every `ACHIEVEMENT` row is the
reward of exactly one achievement.

### The two tables (migration `00021_store_ornaments.sql`, goose up + down)

Migration numbers are reserved **from the tree**, not from a table ([00.overview](00.overview.md) §5.6): as of this
amendment 60 → `00016`, 62 → `00017`, `00018` went out-of-plan, 66 → `00019`, 68 → `00020`, **71 → `00021`**,
72 → `00022`, 74 → `00023`. If the stated number is taken, take the next free one — only the filename moves.

```sql
-- Plan 71 ([P7][P9][I11][U1]). Permanent ownership history: purchase or achievement grant, never
-- expiring, never revoked, never UPDATEd by the system. The insert is ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING,
-- which is also the idempotency guard for the achievement reward leg (plan 75). No `kind` column —
-- kind is derivable from the id prefix and is not stored (ARCHITECTURE §2.9 #3).
CREATE TABLE ornament_ownerships (
    user_id      TEXT NOT NULL,
    ornament_id  TEXT NOT NULL,
    acquired_via TEXT NOT NULL,   -- 'purchase' | 'achievement' — the [P9]/[P11] audit trail
    acquired_at  TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
    PRIMARY KEY (user_id, ornament_id),
    CHECK (acquired_via IN ('purchase', 'achievement'))
);

-- Plan 71 ([P8][P10][I11]). The single source of truth for what the universe wears right now —
-- one row per kind, which is what makes "exactly one applied ornament per kind" structural.
-- PALETTE rows supersede account.palette_preferences (plan 72 migrates and drops it).
-- ABSENCE = the kind's free default; no row is ever written for a default.
CREATE TABLE ornament_selections (
    user_id     TEXT NOT NULL,
    kind        TEXT NOT NULL,   -- 'BACKGROUND' | 'STAR_SHADER' | 'PALETTE'
    ornament_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    selected_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
    PRIMARY KEY (user_id, kind),
    CHECK (kind IN ('BACKGROUND', 'STAR_SHADER', 'PALETTE')),
    -- The id prefix must equal the kind: a PALETTE row can never hold a background id.
    CHECK (ornament_id LIKE lower(kind) || '.%')
);
```

Notes. **No foreign key to `users`**, consistent with every existing product table, so a withdrawn user leaves no
cascade. **No `deleted_at`**: a withdrawn scope is rejected by the `platform` interceptor _before any context
read_ (plan 62), so "hidden during the 30-day window" needs no per-query filter and `store` has no knowledge of
withdrawal at all. **No `params`/`config`/`overrides` column** — that absence is the schema-level half of the [I11]
guard. The `LIKE lower(kind) || '.%'` CHECK is the reason the id prefix is not decoration: it turns "the selection
belongs to its kind" into a schema fact.

### sqlc surface (`db/queries/store/ornaments.sql`, static sqlc only)

- `ListOrnamentOwnerships` — `SELECT ornament_id, acquired_via, acquired_at … WHERE user_id = $1`.
- `ListOrnamentSelections` — `SELECT kind, ornament_id … WHERE user_id = $1`.
- `InsertOrnamentOwnership` — `INSERT … ON CONFLICT (user_id, ornament_id) DO NOTHING`. Plan 72's purchase leg and plan
  75's `OrnamentGranter` both call it; the primary key is the dedup key, so a replayed claim never double-grants.
- `UpsertOrnamentSelection` — `INSERT … ON CONFLICT (user_id, kind) DO UPDATE SET ornament_id = …, selected_at = now()`.
  Plan 72 calls it once per kind inside one transaction ([P8]).
- `DeleteOrnamentOwnershipsForUser` / `DeleteOrnamentSelectionsForUser` — the withdrawal sweep's purge (plan 62).

Every statement is plain sqlc with a conjunctive `user_id` scope; no hand-built dynamic pgx string, because such a query
escapes the [U1] gate entirely (the gate scans only `db/queries/**/*.sql`). `store/pg` maps rows to domain and is
the only importer of `dbgen`.

### Read behavior and the RPC contract (`cosimosi.store.v1`, Connect, unary — §2.7)

```proto
service StoreService {
  rpc GetCatalog(GetCatalogRequest) returns (GetCatalogResponse) { option idempotency_level = NO_SIDE_EFFECTS; }
  rpc GetSelection(GetSelectionRequest) returns (GetSelectionResponse) { option idempotency_level = NO_SIDE_EFFECTS; }
  // rpc Decorate — appended to this same file by plan 72.
}

enum OrnamentKind { ORNAMENT_KIND_UNSPECIFIED = 0; ORNAMENT_KIND_BACKGROUND = 1;
                   ORNAMENT_KIND_STAR_SHADER = 2; ORNAMENT_KIND_PALETTE = 3; }
enum OrnamentAcquisition { ORNAMENT_ACQUISITION_UNSPECIFIED = 0; ORNAMENT_ACQUISITION_FREE = 1;
                           ORNAMENT_ACQUISITION_PURCHASE = 2; ORNAMENT_ACQUISITION_ACHIEVEMENT = 3; }

message GetCatalogRequest {}                    // scope comes from platform.UserScope — never the wire
message Ornament {
  string ornament_id = 1;
  OrnamentKind kind = 2;
  OrnamentAcquisition acquisition = 3;
  int64 price = 4;      // server-resolved from the tier; 0 for FREE and ACHIEVEMENT
  bool owned = 5;
  bool selected = 6;
}
message GetCatalogResponse { repeated Ornament ornaments = 1; }

message GetSelectionRequest {}
message OrnamentSelection { OrnamentKind kind = 1; string ornament_id = 2; }
message GetSelectionResponse { repeated OrnamentSelection selections = 1; }
```

- **No `user_id` field anywhere** — scope comes from `platform.UserScope`. **No `ListInventory`-shaped message and no
  `Equip` method** ([P7]). No label, blurb, asset URL, color, or numeric render parameter — the wire cannot describe an ornament.
- `owned = acquisition == FREE || an ownership row exists`. A `FREE` row is owned by everyone with **no row ever
  written**, which is how [P10]'s free default needs neither a signup grant nor a user backfill.
- `price` is resolved server-side from the row's `Kind` → `values.StoreBackgroundPrice` / `values.StoreStarShaderPrice`
  (`0` for `FREE`/`ACHIEVEMENT` rows and for every palette). The FE never holds the price table,
  which is also what keeps [Z8] true for the demo taster by construction.
- **`GetSelection` always returns exactly three entries, one per kind**, with absence and any unknown/retired id coerced
  to that kind's default. This reproduces `GetPalettePreference`'s shipped contract ("the default id when unset; a
  retired id coerces to the default"), makes the FE boot deterministic, and is what plan 72's
  `account.GetPalettePreference` read-through delegates to.
- Both methods are classified exactly once in `packages/client-cache/src/http-policy.ts` as user-scoped GET with
  `sharedCdn: false`. The production transport hard-fails on a missing, duplicate, or proto-incompatible classification
  before I/O — the structural guard; no runtime check is written (§2.7).
- `store/rpc/server.go` stays thin (map + call, §2.9 #7). `errors.go` declares `ErrScopeRequired`, `ErrStoreRequired`,
  `ErrUnknownOrnamentID` and `ErrOrnamentNotPurchasable` — the last two are the catalog's judgements that plan 72's
  `Decorate` surfaces ([P11]). No new client-facing error reason is added here: the two reads take no input.

### Default ids are contract constants, not values (ARCHITECTURE §4)

`store` declares `DefaultBackgroundOrnamentID`, `DefaultStarShaderOrnamentID` and `DefaultPaletteOrnamentID` as contract
constants, following `account.DefaultPaletteID`'s shipped rationale verbatim ("a contract constant, not a tuning
value"). Each equals the owning registry's **own** default (`DEFAULT_SKY_EFFECT` · `DEFAULT_STAR_SHAPE` ·
`DEFAULT_PALETTE_ID`), so the catalog invents no default. `spec/values.yaml → rendering.active_skin` keeps selecting
the skin whose bloom/camera the scene wears (never sellable); the default background is the `emotion` skin's authored
`sky.effect`, and a test asserts the two agree. (`resolveActiveSkin` already falls back to the product default —
shipped; nothing to fix here.)

The drift guard is the generalized fixture: `apps/api/internal/store/testdata/ornament-ids.json` and its byte-identical
FE twin list **every id the three registries publish** plus the three defaults — the file's length is derived, never
asserted as a number — each side asserting the file matches its own registry, and the FE side additionally asserting
the `emotion` skin's authored `sky.effect` resolves to `DefaultBackgroundOrnamentID`. Plan 72 deletes `account`'s
`registryPaletteIDs` + `palette-ids.json` when it removes the palette write path; until then both fixtures compare
against the same FE source, so neither can drift.

### The renderer selection seams (ARCHITECTURE §3.4)

**`BACKGROUND`.** The selectable id is the as-built `SkyEffectKey` — the single `emotion` skin today hardwires one
authored `sky.effect`, and this unit widens that into the per-user selection `GetSelection` serves. The `SkinKey`
union does **not** grow: skins keep owning bloom/camera (never sellable), and only the enclosed sky effect varies per
user. `setSkinKey` (zero production callers today) is superseded by the effect-level selection seam; the two
app-boundary sites are `apps/web/src/widgets/universe-canvas/ui/UniverseCanvasWidget.tsx` and its mobile twin.

**`STAR_SHADER`.** The registry **already exists** — `packages/3d-renderer/src/assets/bodies/star-shapes.ts`
(`STAR_SHAPES`, `StarShapeKey`, `resolveStarShape`, `createStarShapeBodySource`, default `orb`), authored under plan
57 and still under its design sign-off. This unit plumbs `createStarShapeBodySource(shape)` through
`packages/universe-render/src/StarLayer.tsx` (which today builds only the default body), and a shape change remounts
the layer through the existing `key={\`star-${…}\`}` pattern (`InstancedNodeLayer` owns and disposes the material).
The universe itself stays mounted — plan 14's no-renderer-remount rule holds.

A star shape may vary its form, relief, noise family and a host-timed, `reducedMotion`-honoring surface motion — but
it has **no writable handle** for tint, brightness, seed or the layer-applied scale: those instance attributes are
inputs the builder may only pass through (true of the as-built `StarShapeOptions`, which carries `animate` alone —
this unit pins that as a guard, not a hope). `EMISSIVE_GAIN` is **not** a shape field — raising it lifts a decayed
star back over the bloom threshold, visually undoing forgetting ([V2][F1]). Geometry topology stays within
`rendering.instance_bucket_size`.

`StarShapeKey`/`StarShape` are **rendering vocabulary** (UL §4) and never appear in Go, DB or proto — the server
holds only the opaque `star_shader.*` id, so the render vocabulary cannot flow backward across the §3.4 boundary.

### Golden-parity stance

**No pure function in this unit mirrors TS↔Go, and none should.** There is no formula: a price is a tier→constant lookup
performed once, server-side, and `owned`/`selected` are set membership over rows the server alone holds. The duplicated
artifact is _data_ — the id vocabulary, which must exist in Go (authority, pricing, validation) and in the renderer
(resolution to a node builder). Data cannot drift silently under a byte-identical fixture pair, so the guard is the
generalized `ornament-ids.json` pair rather than a golden math fixture, no `packages/*-logic` mirror is created, and no
price constant needs mirroring at all because the price never crosses into the client.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. A goose migration `00021_store_ornaments.sql` (number reserved from the tree, §5.6) creates `ornament_ownerships (user_id, ornament_id, acquired_via,
acquired_at, PRIMARY KEY (user_id, ornament_id), CHECK (acquired_via IN ('purchase','achievement')))` and
   `ornament_selections (user_id, kind, ornament_id, selected_at, PRIMARY KEY (user_id, kind), CHECK (kind IN
('BACKGROUND','STAR_SHADER','PALETTE')), CHECK (ornament_id LIKE lower(kind) || '.%'))`; `pnpm db:migrate` applies and
   `pnpm db:down` reverses cleanly ([P8][P9][U1]).
2. Neither table has any column for a color, size, brightness, seed, position, or free-form parameter payload (no
   JSON/JSONB column), and neither has a `deleted_at`, expiry, quantity, or revocation column ([V10][I11][P9]).
3. A `PALETTE` selection row cannot hold a background or star-shader id, and a user cannot hold two selections of one
   kind — both refused by the schema, not by application code ([P8][I11]).
4. Every `db/queries/store/*.sql` statement scopes by an owned `user_id`; `pnpm lint:persistence` passes with **no** new
   `platformTables` or `globalQueries` entry ([U1]).
5. `internal/store` follows the context recipe (`{doc.go,types.go,catalog.go,service.go,ports.go,errors.go}` + `pg/` +
   `rpc/`, no empty `domain/`/`app/`), imports no other `internal/` context, and only `store/pg` imports `dbgen`
   (ARCHITECTURE §2.2/§2.3/§2.6).
6. The catalog is an in-code Go table with **one row per id the three registries publish** — no count is declared or
   asserted anywhere; `Ornament` has exactly the fields `{ID, Kind, Acquisition}`, so **no per-row price and no visual
   attribute is representable**, and a duplicate `OrnamentID` fails to compile ([P9][V10][I11]). `OrnamentKind` is closed at exactly
   `BACKGROUND | STAR_SHADER | PALETTE` — the three staging-layer surfaces [P4] opens to customization — so a fourth
   kind cannot appear without amending the Go enum, the DDL `CHECK` and the proto in one reviewed change, and nothing
   meaning-bearing (position, strength, emotion, forgetting, gist) is expressible as a kind at all ([P4][V10][I11]).
7. `BACKGROUND` and `STAR_SHADER` each have exactly one `FREE` row — the owning registry's own default — while
   **every** `PALETTE` row is `FREE` ([P10] as amended: 감정 색은 사고파는 대상이 아니다); a `FREE` row is served as
   `owned = true` with **no** `ornament_ownerships` row ever written for it, and no signup-time grant or user backfill
   exists ([P10]).
8. `ACHIEVEMENT` rows carry no price and report `price = 0`; there are exactly **two** — one background, one star
   shape, never a palette — their ids pinned at job-authoring after plan 57's sign-off; the catalog exposes them as
   unbuyable via `ErrOrnamentNotPurchasable`, and no Go field or proto field names an achievement ([P11]).
9. `proto/cosimosi/store/v1/store.proto` declares `GetCatalog`† and `GetSelection`† with the `ORNAMENT_KIND_*` /
   `ORNAMENT_ACQUISITION_*` enum spellings, contains **no `user_id` field**, **no `ListInventory`-shaped message** and
   **no `Equip` method**, and passes `buf lint` STANDARD and `buf breaking` ([P6][P7]).
10. `GetCatalog` returns **every** catalog row — owned and unowned alike — each with `owned`, `selected` and a
    server-resolved `price` read from `values.StoreBackgroundPrice` / `values.StoreStarShaderPrice` by kind (`0` for
    every `FREE` and `ACHIEVEMENT` row, hence every palette); no price table, label, blurb, or color reaches the

    registries publish** plus the three defaults (the file's length is derived, never asserted as a number), and are

## Policy / Values Impact

- **policy / ux:** the domain rule "**an ornament sells a render-parameter id and nothing else — ownership is permanent
  and never revoked, exactly one ornament is applied per kind, the free default of each kind is the absence of a
  selection row, and no ornament, purchase, or achievement can change a star's position, brightness, size, emotion
  color, seed, bloom, or camera**" will be true once implemented and lands in a NEW
  `spec/policy/domain/ornament-catalog.md`. The companion UX rule — "the catalog is one list of everything; ownership is
  revealed by price alone; there is no inventory, no locker, no equip step" ([P6][P7]) — lands in a NEW
  `spec/policy/ux/decoration.md`, whose panel-facing half plan 73 completes. Both are **written at `/implement-job`; do
  not pre-create.**
- **tech:** the as-built context doc `spec/tech/store-ornaments.md` (NEW) — the `internal/store` recipe, the two tables
  and their queries, the in-code catalog + tier resolution, the id-prefix convention and its DDL CHECK, the
  `GetCatalog`/`GetSelection` contract with the default-resolution rule, the three composition-root adapters, and the
  `ornament-ids.json` drift guard. `spec/tech/rendering.md` gains the two new registry seams (selectable
  `SkyEffectKey`, `STAR_SHAPES` + `createStarShapeBodySource(shape)`, the narrowed meaning of
  `rendering.active_skin`). Both **written at `/implement-job`; do not pre-create.**
- **spec/values.yaml** — the NEW `store` group (sanctioned by [00.overview](00.overview.md) §4: groups are closed,
  keys are not), generated to Go + TS constants by
  `pnpm gen:values`:
