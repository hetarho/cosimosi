# 07. Change: Per-mood emotion color onboarding

> A **change proposal** (a WHAT delta) that modifies the already-shipped behavior of [plan/51.palette-customization](../../plan/51.palette-customization.md).
> Filled by /cosimosi:create-change through a user interview — a reviewable doc, not a one-line prompt.
> The STEPS live in the job that /cosimosi:create-change-job creates. Write it in English.
> Implemented and archived by job 108.

## As-is

Plan 51 built palette customization as a **choice between whole authored sets**.

- `PALETTES` (`packages/emotion/src/registry.ts`) registers two complete thirteen-colour tables under
  stable ids: `cosimosi-default` (the colours job 39 re-authored in OkLCH on the 2D lightness ladder)
  and `muted-dusk` (a softer second table, authored so the registry would have more than one row and
  the swap seam would be provably exercised).
- The stored preference is **one string**. `palette_preferences` (migration `00009`) holds
  `(user_id, palette_id)` — a whole set, chosen or not chosen. A user who never chose owns no row and
  the read derives the default id.
- `setMoodPalette` / `moodColor` is the single swap seam plan 17 left open; an app-layer bootstrap
  applies the stored id at init, and every 2D and 3D consumer reads colour through it.
- `checkPaletteAxisConsistency` scores a **whole palette** for warm/cool agreement with valence
  ([P3]), warn-only, against `palette.axis_warn_valence_threshold`.

Two things about that shipped state matter here.

**No user has ever chosen a colour.** `PaletteSection` exists in `features/change-palette` on both
apps and is **mounted nowhere** — `/me` mounts the profile, account, export, invite and Twinkle rows
and no palette row. So `muted-dusk` is unreachable, the preference table is empty in practice, and
the swap seam has only ever been driven by tests.

**Nothing sits between the nickname and the universe.** Plan 63's profile gate asks for a nickname
and one field only; the next thing a new account sees is a gray latent field. Colour is never
introduced, so the one language the product speaks — a feeling is a hue — arrives unexplained.

## To-be

**A preference is per mood, not per set.** The stored unit is one mood's colour: `(user_id, mood,
color)`, at most thirteen rows for a user, and a mood the user never chose simply owns **no row** and
falls back to the authored default. This is what makes the rest cheap: skipping is the absence of
rows, so there is no "skipped" flag to keep in step with anything, and a per-mood statistic becomes
countable because CALM is a row rather than a field inside somebody's set.

**First sign-in walks one screen, and it is a universe.** After the nickname gate and before the real
universe, a black sky holds thirteen stars, one per mood. Pressing a star opens that feeling's
colour; choosing changes that star under the user's hand, against the other twelve. One screen, not
thirteen steps — the point is to see the feelings **beside each other**, because that is how they
will be seen forever after.

**Three recommendations per mood, honest about what they know.** Each mood offers three candidate
colours. Where the sample behind a candidate is large enough, it carries its share — "41%가 기쁨을 이
색으로 불러요". Where it is not, it carries **no number** and says only that this is a colour usually
given to that feeling. The sample floor is a `spec/values.yaml` number, not a constant in a
component, and below it the ratio is not rendered at all rather than rendered small.

**The statistic is counted over hue buckets, never raw hex.** Every user picks a different hex, so
raw values never accumulate into a share worth showing. Choices are counted into **twelve 30° buckets
on the OkLCH hue circle, plus one near-neutral bucket** for colours below a chroma floor. Chroma and
lightness do not split a bucket — colours a person would call by the same name belong in the same
bucket. A bucket's share is its count over the mood's total, and the colour shown for a bucket is the
most-chosen colour inside it, so the swatch is a real colour somebody picked.

**Hue and chroma are the user's; lightness is the system's.** A stored colour's lightness snaps to the
nearest emotion step on the 2D ladder (`300` / `400` / `500` — L 0.80 / 0.72 / 0.63, [palette.ts](../../../packages/ui/src/palette.ts)).
A star's rendered luminance is its colour's own lightness times its brightness channel, and that
channel belongs to the memory's strength ([V2], plan 24) — so if a mood could be very light or very
dark, two memories of equal strength would read as unequal because of how they felt. The snap keeps
the thirteen colours at one height and leaves the axis the user actually cares about untouched.

**A way out is on screen at every moment.** "나머지는 추천값으로 진행" leaves every untouched mood
without a row and enters the universe. It is not a dismissal of the screen — it is the same thing as
never having pressed those stars.

**A near-duplicate is named as one.** When a chosen colour lands within ΔE (OkLab) of a colour already
given to another mood, the screen says so on the spot. Thirteen feelings have to stay thirteen
colours, and the reader of a nebula cannot un-blend two moods that arrive as one hue.

**The screen consumes the signup signal, and hands the tour on.** Plan 63 publishes a take-once
signup-completion signal and [plan 80](../../plan/80.onboarding-tour.md) consumes it. Absence of colour
rows cannot serve as the trigger, because skipping legitimately leaves none. So the colour screen
rides that same signal — first the colours, then the universe, then the tour — and the handoff must
leave plan 80's trigger intact without introducing a durable "seen" record anywhere.

**The colours are reachable afterwards.** The per-mood editor mounts on `/me`, in the position
`PaletteSection` was built for and never given.

**The aggregate never identifies anyone.** The statistic is counts per (mood, hue bucket) and nothing
else — no user id leaves the aggregate, no individual's table is readable by another account, and the
read exposes no row that could be narrowed to one person.

## Scope / Non-goals (regression boundary)

**In scope**

- The per-mood preference — schema, the get/set contract, and the FE apply through the unchanged
  `setMoodPalette` seam.
- The first-sign-in colour screen on **web and mobile**, its skip, its live thirteen-star preview, and
  its near-duplicate warning.
- The recommendation source — the hue-bucket aggregate, its sample floor, and the no-number wording
  below the floor.
- The lightness snap onto the emotion steps of the 2D ladder.
- Mounting the per-mood editor on `/me`.
- `muted-dusk` as a **recommendation set** rather than a stored preference: its ANGER↔STRESS collision
  (ΔE 0.024 — the two read as one colour, found while measuring in job 39) must be resolved or the set
  withdrawn before it can be offered as a recommendation.

**Must not change (the regression boundary)**

- **The swap seam.** `moodColor` / `setMoodPalette` stays the only path by which colour reaches a 2D
  chrome or a 3D body ([P2][I11]). Rendering stays palette-agnostic; no layer learns that a preference
  exists.
- **The meaning layer.** `Emotion`'s mood / valence / arousal / intensity and the mood→valence
  coordinates are untouched. Colour is downstream of meaning and never writes back ([I3][I11]).
- **The brightness channel.** `EffectiveBrightness` remains the only thing that sets a star's
  brightness ([V2]). A colour choice may not reach it.
- **The axis-consistency check stays warn-only.** It may inform a choice; it may never block one ([P3]).
- **The default table.** `cosimosi-default` — job 39's thirteen colours — remains the fallback for
  every mood without a row, unchanged.
- **The nickname step's shape.** It stays one field, still a gate rather than a route (plan 63). The
  colour screen is a separate surface after it, not a second field inside it.
- **Plan 80's vocabulary.** The tour still may not write and still owns no "seen" record; the colour
  screen is not a tour step.
- **Free.** No ornament, no purchase, no Twinkle spend — palettes stay free ([P10]).

**Non-goals**

- No palette sharing, following, or any social surface over the aggregate.
- No achievement, unlock, or ornament gating of any colour.
- No re-authoring of the default thirteen colours (job 39 / plan 57 owns them).
- No change to the 3D bodies, the sky, or the nebula's blending.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. A user's emotion colours are stored **per mood**; a mood the user never chose has no stored row and
   renders the authored default colour.
2. Skipping the screen stores **nothing** — no rows, and no durable "skipped" or "seen" record anywhere.
3. The first-sign-in colour screen appears **after the nickname gate and before the universe**, on web
   and mobile, and shows thirteen stars — one per mood — against a bare night sky.
4. Choosing a colour updates that mood's star **live** on the same screen, beside the other twelve.
5. Each mood offers **three** recommendations. A recommendation whose bucket sample is at or above the
   `spec/values.yaml` floor shows its share; one below the floor shows **no ratio at all**.
6. The aggregate counts choices into twelve 30° OkLCH hue buckets plus one near-neutral bucket; two
   colours in the same bucket count as one preference, and a bucket's displayed swatch is the
   most-chosen colour within it.
7. The aggregate exposes counts only — no user id, and no read that resolves to an individual account.
8. A stored colour's lightness lands on one of the emotion steps of the 2D ladder (L 0.80 / 0.72 /
   0.63); its hue and chroma are exactly what the user chose.
9. Choosing a colour within the configured ΔE (OkLab) of a colour already given to another mood raises
   an on-screen notice, and does not block the choice.
10. "나머지는 추천값으로 진행" is reachable at every moment of the screen and enters the universe
    immediately.
11. The per-mood editor is reachable from `/me` after onboarding, and edits apply through the unchanged
    `setMoodPalette` seam with no `GetUniverse` refetch.
12. Every tuning number the change introduces lives in `spec/values.yaml` — none is hardcoded in a
    component, a query, or a Go handler.
13. No regression: rendering remains palette-agnostic, the meaning layer and the brightness channel are
    untouched, the axis check remains warn-only, the nickname gate keeps its shape, and plan 80's
    take-once tour trigger still fires on a first sign-in.
